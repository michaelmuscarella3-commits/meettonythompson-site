THIS WEEK’S JOURNAL

People rarely leave a job for a single reason.

 

Most resignations trace back to one thing — a breakdown in trust, growth, or connection.

 
1

Growth & Stagnation

 

Limited Career Paths

People disengage when they see no path to advance.

Stalled Development

They’ve outgrown the role — with no new challenge, learning, or upskilling.

 
2

Leadership & Management

 

Poor Management

“People don’t quit jobs, they quit bosses.” Micromanagement, favoritism, and ego push people away.

No Psychological Safety

When people don’t feel safe to speak up or fail, they disengage long before they resign.

 
3

Toxic Culture & Environment

 

Disrespect & Betrayal

One-sided expectations — constant hustle without security or care — breed resentment.

Exclusion & Poor Communication

Cliques, gossip, and no transparency leave people feeling undervalued and disconnected.

 
4

Burnout & Well-Being

 

Work-Life Imbalance

“Always-on” pressure leads to burnout. Many now choose flexibility over higher pay.

Lack of Recognition

When effort goes unnoticed and work feels purposeless, motivation declines.

 
5

Compensation & Insecurity

 

Inadequate Pay

Pay becomes the issue when raises don’t reflect performance, market value, or rising costs.

Unstable Organizations

Uncertainty, budget cuts, or layoffs push people toward stability elsewhere.

 
 

THE BOTTOM LINE

People don’t usually quit suddenly.

They leave once they realize the organization stopped investing in them, listening to them, and growing with them.
