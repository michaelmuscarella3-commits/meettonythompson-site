THIS WEEK’S JOURNAL

There are three levels of thought leadership — but only one attracts the best clients.

 

A few years ago, a founder spent two hours teaching me how to close a sale. The frameworks were solid, the flowcharts polished, the presentation clearly well-rehearsed. By the end, I understood the method — but I didn’t leave feeling I’d been listening to someone truly exceptional.

That’s when something clicked.

 

Level 1 — The Enthusiast

 

Genuinely loves the topic. Passionate, well-read, happy to explain how things work. The problem? So can AI — and so can thousands of others publishing nearly identical content.

“Here’s how to close a sale” might still be useful. But usefulness alone isn’t enough anymore.

In 2026, that kind of content has become interchangeable.

 

Level 2 — The Expert

 

“Here’s how to close a sale.”

“Here’s how I closed a sale.”

That one word changes everything — now you’re speaking from lived experience, not borrowed knowledge. This is where genuine thought leadership begins. Stories matter. Specific experiences matter. Evidence matters.

When I started sharing real examples from my own career instead of just teaching frameworks, people trusted the message far more — not because the advice changed, but because the proof did.

It builds credibility and an audience — but it’s rarely enough to become the obvious choice.

 

Level 3 — Their Expert

 

Not “the” expert. “Their” expert. That two-letter shift does almost all the work. “The expert” proves you’re good. “Their expert” gives your audience a reason to choose you specifically — and that comes through specificity.

“Here’s how I closed a sale.”

“Here’s how I closed a sale with Deloitte.”

The authority gap is enormous.

A marketing consultant.

Someone who ran campaigns for HP and the London Stock Exchange.

A career coach.

Someone whose clients landed roles at JP Morgan and McKinsey.

No household names? That’s fine — transformation works just as well. Helping someone grow from $500 a month to $2,000 a month is evidence, not opinion.

 

It Shows Up Everywhere

 

These levels don’t just apply to content — they show up in sales conversations, networking, discovery calls, and client meetings.

Level 2 feels comfortable; nobody questions your personal experience. Level 3 requires stronger proof — the case study, the numbers, the names, the transformation. That’s exactly why so few people do it.

 
 

THE SHIFT

As AI fills the internet with polished advice, generic authority loses value every day.

Specific authority wins — because people can ignore another opinion, but they rarely ignore credible evidence.

 

Keep chasing excellence.
